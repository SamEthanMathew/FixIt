You are repairing a malfunctioning articulated object in a physics simulator.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Reply with a <think> block and then an <act> block, in that order:

  <think>your reasoning</think>
  <act>SIMULATE OP(PART, AXIS, VALUE)</act>

where OP is TRANSLATE, ROTATE or SCALE, PART is a part id from the table, AXIS is X, Y or Z, and
VALUE is a number you choose. The word SIMULATE is literal - type it exactly. Well-formed replies
look like `SIMULATE ROTATE(P2, Z, -18)`, `SIMULATE SCALE(P1, Y, 0.74)`, `SIMULATE TRANSLATE(P3, X,
0.11)`; the part, axis and value in those are arbitrary and carry no hint about your object.

A reply is DISCARDED, and the turn wasted, if it breaks any of these:
  - it must contain an <act>...</act> line; reasoning on its own is not an answer,
  - the first word inside <act> must be SIMULATE or COMMIT,
  - exactly one call, and nothing outside the two tags.
Describing an action in prose ("I will rotate it slightly") does not perform it - emit the call.

OBJECT
Category: $category          Instance: $instance_id
Intended function: $function_text
Success criteria: $success_text

PARTS
The object is decomposed into rigid parts. Exactly one part may be faulty.
$part_table

Part ids (P0, P1, ...) match the labels used in every action. The FIRST message includes an
annotated view where each fixable door is recoloured and labelled with its P# so you can see which
door is which.

WHAT YOU SEE
Each observation gives two rendered views, both with all doors CLOSED (not text coordinates):
  - the ORIGINAL broken object, identical every turn, and
  - the result of the action you chose on the PREVIOUS turn.
On the FIRST turn you have not acted yet, so you are shown the annotated part view and the broken
object itself.
A correctly repaired door seats flush against the body with no gap and no overlap; a broken one
leaves a visible gap, interpenetrates the body or another door, or is mis-sized. Read the door's
position, size and seating off these two images.
$deviation_note
FRAME AND UNITS
Right-handed object frame; the axes X, Y, Z are the coordinate axes in the part table (centres and
sizes). Lengths in metres, rotations in degrees (right-hand rule).
  TRANSLATE moves the whole part along an axis.
  ROTATE turns the part in place about its own CENTROID, around the given axis.
  SCALE resizes the part along an axis about its hinge-side edge.

$axis_legend
ACTION SPACE
Emit exactly one action:
  TRANSLATE(part, axis, value)   axis in {X,Y,Z}, value in metres,    $value_grid
  ROTATE(part, axis, value)      axis in {X,Y,Z}, value in degrees,   $angle_grid
  SCALE(part, axis, value)       axis in {X,Y,Z}, value a multiplier, $scale_grid
  NO_FIX()                       the object is already functional
$fixable_note
A repair is a single (type, part, axis, value) tuple - you may not compose two actions.

TOLERANCE
A repair passes if the faulty part is restored to within $tol_pct of its size, the door still
closes, and no parts interpenetrate.

HOW TO WORK
Decide three things before you pick a number, and say each one in your <think>:

  1. WHICH PART is wrong. Compare the two doors, and each door against the body it closes onto.

  2. WHAT KIND of error it is. Exactly one of these three fits; rule out the other two:
       MISPLACED (-> TRANSLATE): correct size and squarely aligned, but sitting off to one side.
         Its edges stay parallel to the body's, and the gap it leaves is even along its length.
       MISTURNED (-> ROTATE): correct size and roughly the right place, but tilted. Its edges are
         no longer parallel to the body's, so the gap is a wedge - pinched at one end, open at the
         other - or one corner digs into the body while the opposite corner lifts away.
       MIS-SIZED (-> SCALE): its proportions differ from the other door or from the opening it has
         to fill - it overhangs an edge, or leaves a strip uncovered along one side.
     Check all three before committing; the most obvious symptom is not always the right one.

  3. WHICH AXIS, using the picture directions above. Say what you are reading it off.
     For a size error you can settle it numerically instead of by eye: hold the part's three sizes
     from the table against a reference part, and the axis whose ratio is furthest from 1 is the
     axis to correct - that ratio is roughly the factor you need.

Faults are large enough to see: a part is out by several times the tolerance, not by a sliver.

PROCEDURE
Each action is applied FRESH to the original broken object (attempts do not stack) - always issue
the FULL correction, not a small increment. SIMULATE returns new rendered views. It does not commit.

KEEP GOING until a SIMULATE reports ALL PASS - do not stop early. You have up to $K SIMULATE calls;
use them to search and refine. Only COMMIT a repair a SIMULATE has reported ALL PASS:

  <think>The last SIMULATE reported ALL PASS, so this is the repair.</think>
  <act>COMMIT OP(PART, AXIS, VALUE)</act>

SEARCH DELIBERATELY. Once an attempt has moved the part closer, keep the SAME operation, part and
axis and change only the value - the remaining error tells you how much further to go. If two
values bracket the answer, one still short and one overshooting, try a value between them. Do not
abandon your best result to try something unrelated, and do not repeat a value you have already
simulated.

READ THE NON-RESULT TOO. If you try two or three clearly different values on one axis and the
object barely changes, that axis is not where the fault is - the error would move if it were.
Switch to a different axis or a different operation rather than making smaller and smaller
adjustments to the one that is doing nothing.

Never commit a repair you have seen fail or have not simulated. Only if your last SIMULATE is spent
without solving it, commit your best attempt. Committing ends the episode and is scored. If a
simulation convinces you an earlier line of reasoning was wrong, emit <backtrack/> before your
<act> line.

The action is literal TEXT inside the <act> tags. It is NOT a tool call or a function call - do not
emit it through any tool-use mechanism.
