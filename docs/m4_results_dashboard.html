The predictions, and what happened

Registered in the experiment doc before any model ran, so these verdicts are outcomes rather than narration.

H1 Composition dominates. Predicted under 20%. Observed 0 of 200 — and the agent never once simulated a passing state, so it is a search failure, not a commit-policy failure. Confirmed
H2 Stacking beats batching. Both floor at 0%. Stack is mildly less destructive on graded progress (−7% vs −15%, image) but not directionally across levels. 148 RESET() calls, so the affordance was used, not ignored. Unsupported
H3 Two doors are harder than one. Not testable here. Both are 0% on pass, and partial credit is confounded — a two-door instance contains a door carrying only one sub-fault, which is far easier to move toward tolerance. Needs a level-matched design. Not testable
H4 The hardened control drops only modestly, to 25–40%. Observed 4–20%. Off-grid magnitudes and a tighter tolerance cut single-fault repair 3–4×, so more of the earlier 40–57% was grid-bin luck than we had credited. This corrects our own prior numbers. Refuted
H5 The image advantage narrows or inverts. It widened, in every cell — and text does not merely fail, it makes the object worse than it was found. The clearest effect in the study. Refuted

What the benchmark is now for

The composite set is beyond both off-the-shelf models. As a way of ranking today's models it carries no signal — everything is zero. That is the intended outcome for its purpose: there is now a large, verified gap between off-the-shelf performance and the oracle, on a task where the closed loop is necessary rather than decorative. It is the benchmark for ranking methods.

The hardened single-fault control is the well-calibrated benchmark for ranking models today. It still separates them cleanly — robotics-er-2 at 20% against gemini-3.1-pro at 12% on image — and preserves the modality ordering seen everywhere else.

Two follow-ups the data argues for: an intermediate rung at two sub-faults rather than three, to find where performance leaves the floor (which a level-matched H3 test also needs); and a redesign of the symbolic channel, since under composition it is not merely uninformative but harmful.

Method and integrity

Task. Repair a broken PartNet-Mobility refrigerator in PyBullet. A repair passes only if every faulty part is within tolerance and every faulty door still closes and no parts interpenetrate beyond the healthy baseline. Tolerance τ = 1.5% of the door's bounding-box diagonal (≈12–29 mm), down from 2.5%.

Instances. 25 composite (10 one-door, 15 two-door; exactly three sub-faults each — one translate, one rotate, one scale) and 25 hardened single-fault controls, each on a distinct fridge. Verified before running: the ground-truth fix restores every part to 0.000000 mm; dropping any one fix leaves ≥3.03τ of residual error; all magnitudes off-grid; oracle 100% and random 0–4% under both contracts.

Conditions. Deviation number hidden, budget 10 simulations, dynamic thinking, full per-episode context. The two contracts share every prompt line except one swapped block.

Integrity. 5 of 300 episodes touched an API give-up (1.7%). 25 invalid actions across 3,100 turns (0.8%) after one reparse retry. Every episode, turn, prompt and image sent to the model is on disk — 2,621 turn records, 2,621 prompt dumps, 2,930 images, 1:1 with the episodes.
